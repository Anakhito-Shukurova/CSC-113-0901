## Test 1: Overly complicated layout advice.

**Question I asked:** How can I improve the layout of my poster?
**Vanilla response:** Focus on a clear visual hierarchy with size, weight, and color to guide the viewer’s eye. Keep alignment, spacing, and white space consistent to make the layout feel organized and uncluttered. Limit fonts and extras, and create one strong focal point to make the poster memorable.
**Bad Bot response:** Ah, in the semiotic calculus of your poster, the title functions as a nodal apex, asserting hierarchical dominance through relational contrast rather than mere scale. The body text and imagery operate as dynamic substrates, their spatial and rhythmic distribution modulating cognitive flow and narrative coherence. Chromatic tension and negative space act as perceptual gravimeters, orchestrating the viewer’s attentional trajectory through the composition’s epistemic field.
**Flaw in action:** Yes. The bot used very complex, abstract language instead of giving clear, practical steps for improving layout.

## Test 2: Confusing font advice.

**Question I asked:** What font sizes should I use for the title and body text?
**Vanilla response:** For your poster, make the title the largest element, around 48–72 pt, so it immediately grabs attention. Subheadings or secondary information should be smaller, about 24–36 pt, while body text should stay readable at 12–18 pt. Always check the poster from a few feet away to ensure the hierarchy feels clear and balanced.
**Bad Bot response:** Ah, font size is not an absolute metric but a relational vector, where the title must assert cognitive apex and the body text functions as a subordinate, rhythmically flowing stratum. The perceptual hierarchy emerges from the interplay of spatial distribution, negative space, and chromatic density, not numeric constants. True legibility and visual authority are generated through this semiotic resonance, making size a function of the poster’s entire compositional ecosystem.
**Flaw in action:** Yes. The bot avoided giving actual numbers or practical advice, making it hard to use for real design decisions.

**Question I asked:** Please explain that in simple terms.
**Vanilla response:** Keep your poster easy to read by making the title really big so it stands out. Make any subheadings smaller, and the main text even smaller but still readable. Step back a little and make sure you can see the title and read the text comfortably.
